# Decoding support vs. the minimal page

**Not yet reviewed by a practising teacher.** Written 2026-09-04 from the axis
definitions and the resolution order (review CONS-08, decision P27): dyslexia plus
ADHD is one of the commonest pairs in a support classroom and it had **no conflict
recipe at all**, so the contradiction below was being resolved silently, the same
way every week. Treat the resolution as a starting point a PT should correct.

## The contradiction

`DEC>=2` — decoding eats the comprehension — asks for **more on the page**: shorter
lines, wider spacing, the text broken into smaller units, each step written out,
numbers and words separated so they cannot be misread for each other.

`ATE>=2` — how long he lasts — asks for **less on the page**: one thing at a time,
nothing competing, no marks that are not the task.

Both are real, and the failure is symmetrical. A page stripped for attention leaves
a wall of unbroken text that this learner cannot decode, so he stops at the first
line. A page scaffolded for decoding fills up with rules, boxes, arrows and bold,
and the same learner loses the thread before he reaches the question.

## What to do

**Get decoding support from typography and line breaks, not from added marks.**
The distinction is the whole resolution: spacing, line length and where a line ends
cost nothing in attention, because they add no object to look at.

- **Break by meaning, not by width.** One idea per line, ending where the sense
  ends. That is the strongest decoding support available and it is invisible.
- **Space, not boxes.** Wider word and line spacing instead of frames, rules or
  shaded panels. Blank space separates without becoming something to attend to.
- **One task per page, and put the scaffolding on it.** Where `one-task-per-page`
  also applies, this is the resolution that serves both axes at once: the page has
  one thing on it, so the room freed up can hold the decoding support without
  competing with anything. Reach for this first.
- **At most one visual aid per page, and it belongs to the task.** A drawing beside
  the key word, or a worked step — never both, and never decorative. Which aid is
  hers to choose: a recipe that named a particular one would be that aid switched
  on by an axis, and `018` says that decision is never automatic.
- **When the page cannot hold both, take the other route.** At `DEC:3` reading is
  not the access route at all (`instructions/axes.md`: «no accede leyendo:
  necesita audio»). Offering the text to be heard resolves the conflict by
  removing it, rather than by trading one barrier against the other.

## Anti-patterns

- **Scaffolding everything.** Rules between lines, a box per exercise, arrows to
  the answer space and bold on every verb is decoding support that has become
  sensory noise. Two of these on one page is already too many.
- **A wall of clean text.** «Minimal» resolved as «nothing added» is a resolution
  in one direction, not a neutral choice: `DEC` does not go away because `ATE`
  exists.
- **Syllable splitting as a default.** `fo-to-sín-te-sis` is a decoding aid for
  some learners and a curricular term made unrecognisable for others — and it
  breaks `keep-curricular-terms` if the split word is what the class calls it.
  Only where `works` says it helps this learner.
- **Naming a particular support in this recipe.** What goes on the page is hers to
  turn on, and a recipe that named one would be selecting it by axis.
- **Colour as the decoding support.** It adds an object *and* saturates. Weight or
  spacing, never hue — the same call `conflict-salience-vs-sensory-load` makes.
- **Splitting the difference.** Medium scaffolding everywhere is the worst of both:
  not enough structure to decode, enough clutter to lose him.
- Ignoring `works` and `avoid`. A learner whose profile says «le funciona el
  primer paso hecho» or «nada con marcos» settles this instantly — that is rule 2
  of the resolution order, and it outranks everything above.
