# Salience vs. sensory load

## The contradiction

`ATE>=2` calls for salience: highlight the key word, colour the instruction, make
the important thing jump off the page. `REG>=2` calls for the opposite: reduce
stimulation, mute the palette, keep the page calm and predictable.

These are directly opposed and both are real. Getting it wrong in either direction
fails: a flat page loses the learner's attention, an emphatic one saturates them
and can end the session.

## What to do

**Get salience from structure and scarcity, not from intensity.**

- **Position over colour.** The important thing goes first, alone, on its own
  line. Position is salient and costs nothing sensorially.
- **One emphasis per page. Exactly one.** Scarcity is what makes emphasis work;
  five highlights are wallpaper, and wallpaper is load.
- **Weight, not hue.** Bold on a single word is far quieter than a colour block
  and reads as strongly.
- **Space as the separator.** Blank space isolates without adding anything.
- **Predictable placement.** The instruction is always in the same place. A
  learner who knows where to look does not need to be attracted there — this is
  the resolution that serves both axes at once, and it should be the first reach.

Where colour is genuinely needed, take one muted accent and use it for one
purpose consistently across every sheet. Never more than one, and never a
different one next week.

## Anti-patterns

- **Splitting the difference into medium-strength emphasis everywhere.** The worst
  of both: not salient enough to guide, present enough to saturate.
- **Highlighter yellow, or any saturated fill.** Reads as an alarm.
- **Emphasis that moves between sheets.** Predictability is a support for both
  axes; inconsistency destroys it for both.
- **Removing all emphasis "to be safe."** `ATE` does not go away because `REG`
  exists. A flat page is a resolution in one direction, not a neutral choice.
- Ignoring `avoid`. A learner with a documented colour trigger settles this
  instantly — that is rule 2 of the resolution order.
