# Signpost the page

## What this axis is actually about

`EJE` is not about understanding. «Necesita los pasos dados» and «no arranca sin el
primero hecho» describe a learner who **cannot start**, and who cannot tell whether
he is nearly done. Nothing about the content is too hard for him.

So this recipe changes almost nothing about the words. It answers three questions
the page does not answer: *how much is this, where do I start, and how do I know I
have finished.*

Separate from `chunk-the-prose` on purpose. That one is `COG` — a dense paragraph
carrying four ideas. This one is `EJE` — a page with no visible end. The same
learner often needs both, and `axes:` is AND, so one recipe demanding both axes
would fire for neither learner alone (see `recipes/README.md`).

## What to do

**Say how much it is, at the top, before anything else.** «Esto son tres partes» or
«Son cuatro preguntas». A page whose end he cannot see is a page he does not begin —
and the number costs one line.

**Number the parts, and keep the numbers visible.** Not decoration: it is how he
answers «where was I» after being interrupted, which he will be.

**Say what to do first, in one line.** «Empieza por la parte 1: sólo hay que
leerla.» The first step being *obviously small* is the whole trick — `EJE: 3` is
«no arranca sin el primero hecho», so the first thing must be something he can
finish before deciding whether he can do the rest.

**Put a place to mark progress.** A box beside each part. Ticking three boxes is
self-monitoring made external, which is exactly what this axis needs and what a
plain page withholds.

**Keep the original numbering where the material had it** — the class works out
loud on «la pregunta cinco», the same rule `one-task-per-page` states. Signposting
adds a layer; it does not renumber underneath it.

## In an exam, only half of this

Scope includes `instruction`, so this now applies to an exam paper — and the
baseline snapshot shows it firing there beside `exam-access-not-difficulty`, which
outranks it. Two of the five things above are **not** allowed on a paper:

- **No «empieza por la 1».** Telling a candidate where to start changes how the
  paper is taken. On a worksheet it is the whole point; on an exam it is the
  invigilator's business or nobody's.
- **No progress boxes.** A tick beside a question is a mark somebody has to
  interpret when the paper is marked, and `012`'s checklist forbids adding
  scaffolding to an exam.

What **is** allowed, and is genuinely access rather than difficulty: saying how many
questions there are, and keeping their numbers visible. «Son seis preguntas» does
not change a single thing that is asked.

## Before

> **Los ríos de España**
>
> Los ríos de la vertiente atlántica son largos y de caudal regular… *(media
> página)* … mientras que los de la vertiente mediterránea son cortos y muy
> irregulares… *(media página)* … El Ebro es la excepción… *(media página)*

## After

> **Los ríos de España**
>
> *Son tres partes. Empieza por la 1: sólo hay que leerla.*
>
> **☐ 1 · Los ríos atlánticos**
>
> Los ríos de la vertiente atlántica son largos y de caudal regular…
>
> **☐ 2 · Los ríos mediterráneos**
>
> … son cortos y muy irregulares…
>
> **☐ 3 · El Ebro, que es la excepción**
>
> El Ebro…
>
> *Cuando tengas las tres marcadas, has terminado.*

The text is the same text. What changed is that he can see how much there is, which
part he is in, and when he has finished.

## Anti-patterns

**Signposting instead of chunking.** Three headings over three half-pages of dense
prose is a page he can now navigate and still cannot read. If `COG` is also set,
`chunk-the-prose` applies too and neither substitutes for the other.

**A first step that is not small.** «Empieza por la parte 1» over half a page of
text is the same page with a label on it. If part 1 is big, the parts are wrong.

**Renumbering to make the signposting tidy.** The class says «la cinco». A page
where five became two is a page he cannot follow out loud, and this recipe is not
allowed to buy tidiness with that.

**Progress boxes that are the answer boxes.** He ticks «done» in the place he was
meant to write, or writes in the place he was meant to tick. One box per part, in
the margin, visibly not a place to answer.

**«Esto son tres partes» when it is four.** The count is a promise. Getting it wrong
is worse than not making it, because he stops at three.

**Adding a «resumen» that replaces reading.** That is `chunk-the-prose`'s
anti-pattern and it applies here for the same reason: this axis is not about
understanding less, it is about being able to start.
