# Keep curricular terms

## What to do

This is a **guard**, not a transformation: it constrains every other
simplification recipe.

When simplifying language, identify the terms that are themselves the curricular
content — the words the learner is supposed to leave the unit knowing. Keep them.
Simplify everything around them, and add a short definition alongside on first
use.

If in doubt whether a term is curricular, ask: would the teacher accept an answer
that never uses this word? If no, the word stays.

## Before

> Los organismos **autótrofos** son capaces de **sintetizar** su propia materia
> orgánica a partir de sustancias inorgánicas.

## After — correct

> Los seres vivos **autótrofos** fabrican su propio alimento.
> *Autótrofo = que se fabrica su propia comida.*
>
> Lo fabrican a partir de cosas que no están vivas, como el agua y el aire.

## After — wrong

> Algunos seres vivos fabrican su propia comida.

The second version reads beautifully and has removed the curriculum. The learner
cannot now answer "¿qué es un ser vivo autótrofo?", which is the question the
unit is assessed on. This is the failure mode this recipe exists to prevent.

## Anti-patterns

- **Replacing the term with a synonym** — the exact failure above.
- **Keeping the term but dropping the definition**, leaving the sentence shorter
  and equally impenetrable.
- **Defining it in language harder than the term.** "Autótrofo: organismo con
  capacidad de biosíntesis" helps nobody.
- **Defining it every time it appears.** Once on first use, then a glossary block
  at the end.
- Treating *every* long word as curricular. "Sintetizar" above is not the content
  — "autótrofo" is. Simplify the first, keep the second.
