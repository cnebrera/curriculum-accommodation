# When decoding eats the comprehension

**Not yet reviewed by a practising teacher.** Written 2026-09-04 from
`instructions/axes.md` and the gap the review found (PROD-01, decision P1): a
typical dyslexia profile — `DEC` 2 or 3 with `LIN` at 0 or 1 — selected **nothing at
all**, because every recipe that would have helped needed a second axis as well.
`lectura-facil-es` asks for `DEC>=2` *and* `LIN>=2`; `keep-curricular-terms` is a
constraint rather than an adaptation. So the commonest reason a PT opens this
application produced a sheet almost identical to the original. Treat what follows as
a starting point a PT should correct.

## What this axis is, and what it is not

`DEC>=2` is «descifrar le come la comprensión». He can read — that is the point. The
letters cost him so much that nothing is left for what the sentence means, so a text
he could understand if it were read aloud defeats him on paper.

It is **not** `LIN`. The language is not the problem: if you simplify the vocabulary
of a text this learner cannot decode, you have made it shorter and no more readable
to him, and you may have taken out the curricular word he is supposed to be
learning. Simplify the *page*, not the language.

## What to do

- **Break lines by meaning.** One idea per line, ending where the sense ends, rather
  than wherever the margin falls. This is the single strongest thing available and it
  changes not one word of the content.
- **Shorter lines.** A narrower measure means fewer chances to lose the place
  mid-line, which is where the re-reading starts.
- **More space between lines and between words.** Crowded text is harder to track;
  space costs nothing but paper.
- **Never a wall.** Any paragraph over about four lines gets a break, even mid-idea
  if the alternative is a block he will not start.
- **Keep the curricular word and give it a foothold** — bold on first use, or the
  same word always in the same place — rather than replacing it. Replacing it breaks
  `keep-curricular-terms` and costs him the word the class is using.
- **At `DEC:3`, reading is not the access route at all.** `axes.md` says it plainly:
  «no accede leyendo: necesita audio». The sheet still goes out, and it goes out
  *with* the audio-ready version (`019`) rather than as a page he cannot enter.

## Before

> Las plantas son organismos autótrofos que fabrican su propio alimento mediante la
> fotosíntesis, un proceso que se produce en los cloroplastos y que requiere luz
> solar, agua y dióxido de carbono.

## After

> Las plantas fabrican su propio alimento.
>
> Lo hacen con la **fotosíntesis**.
>
> Para la fotosíntesis necesitan tres cosas:
> - luz del sol
> - agua
> - dióxido de carbono

## Anti-patterns

- **Simplifying the language instead of the page.** That is `LIN`'s recipe, and
  applied here it removes content without removing the barrier.
- **Taking out the curricular term.** «Fotosíntesis» is what he is being taught. A
  sheet that says «cómo comen las plantas» has adapted the wrong thing.
- **Syllable splitting by default.** `fo-to-sín-te-sis` helps some learners and makes
  a curricular word unrecognisable for others. Only where `works` says it helps
  *this* learner.
- **A different typeface per sheet.** Whatever the font, it has to be the same one
  every week: recognising the shape of the page is itself a support.
- **Reading it aloud instead of adapting it.** Both, where he needs both. «Se lo leo
  yo» is a person's time, and it is not available for homework.
