# The response route

## What to do

`MOT` says nothing about what the learner knows. It says **how they can answer** —
and until this recipe existed, a profile with `MOT>=2` selected nothing for that:
the teacher set the axis and nothing changed.

Change the response space, and only the response space. What is being asked does
not move.

**Read `response` in the profile first.** She has written there how this learner
answers — dictating, typing, pointing, out loud. That outranks any assumption
below.

**Dictates or answers aloud.** Remove the ruled lines. A third of the page in
rules nobody will use makes the sheet look longer than it is, and tells the
learner something was expected of him that he cannot do. Leave a small marked
space, so whoever collects the sheet can see it was answered even though nothing
is written.

**Types.** Do not lay the page out for handwriting. The sheet carries the
question; the answer lives elsewhere. Number the questions so he can refer to one
without copying it.

**Points or chooses.** Turn an open answer into a choice **without changing the
demand**: the options must contain the right answer and distractors that still
require knowing the same thing. Three options, not five, and none absurd — an
option ruled out by being absurd is an easier question. Space the targets so a
correct answer cannot be a lucky one.

**Writes slowly but writes.** Do not remove the writing. Reduce *how much*: one
word instead of a sentence where the word shows the same understanding. Say so in
the instruction, so he does not think he has misread it.

## Before

> **4.** Explica con tus palabras por qué las plantas necesitan la luz del sol.
>
> ____________________________________________
>
> ____________________________________________
>
> ____________________________________________

## After

*(profile says `response: { default: "dicta" }`)*

> **4.** Explica por qué las plantas necesitan la luz del sol.
>
> *Contesta en voz alta.*
>
> ☐ Contestado

The question is unchanged. Three lines of ruled space became one box that records
that it was answered.

## Anti-patterns

**Filling the page with lines "just in case".** That is what the original does,
and it is precisely what this axis flags as the barrier.

**Removing the answer space entirely.** With no mark that an answer belongs there,
a corrected sheet cannot say whether the learner responded — and in an exam that
is a question left unassessed.

**Turning everything into multiple choice.** The easy way out, and it changes what
is assessed: recognising is not producing. It is also the change most likely to
look fine to a reviewer and be wrong.

**Choosing the route from the diagnosis.** The profile says `MOT: 2`; it does not
say why. A learner with cerebral palsy, one with a broken wrist and one with
dysgraphia share the axis and share no solution. The one that works is in
`response`, written by the person who teaches him.

**Treating it as difficulty in an exam.** A changed response route is an access
arrangement and must be named as one in the report (hard rule 5). Quietly
simplifying the question instead is a different exam.
