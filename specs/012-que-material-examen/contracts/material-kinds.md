# Contract — a material kind

For whoever adds the fifth. A kind is worth existing **only if it forbids
something** — otherwise it is a label, and the application has enough of those.

## What the author of a kind promises

1. **Say what it forbids, in one clause.** «No cambies las cantidades ni las
   operaciones que se practican.» If you cannot write that clause, the kind does
   not exist yet.
2. **The label is her words, not a category.** «Una hoja de problemas», not
   `problem_set`. She picks from this list in under two seconds or the question is
   friction.
3. **The rule is written to the model and about the material** — never about the
   learner. Who the learner is comes from the profile (`011`); this is about what
   is on the paper.
4. **Do not overlap.** Two kinds whose prohibitions are the same are one kind with
   two names, and she will pick between them at random.

## What the application promises

1. **The kind is asked, never inferred, never defaulted.** The same reasoning as
   the correction-scope question: guessing is the failure, and a default is a
   guess with better manners.
2. **Her statement is carried, not overridden.** If the document looks like
   something else, the report says so and nothing changes. She may be adapting
   last year's exam as practice.
3. **The rule reaches the model verbatim**, alongside the hard rules, which still
   outrank it.
4. **No code change** to add one.

## The four that ship

| id | Forbids |
|---|---|
| `worksheet` | The curricular demand; the original numbering |
| `exam` | **The demand of any question** (`001` FR-011) |
| `study` | The content — its failure is summarising the curriculum away |
| `problems` | **The quantities and the operations they exercise** |

## Why `problems` is not `worksheet`

Because its failure is the least visible in this system. A teacher reading an
adapted maths sheet sees plausible problems and has no reason to check whether the
numbers still exercise carrying — and a model asked to make something easier
reaches for the numbers first, since that is what "easier" means everywhere else.

Changing 47 × 8 to 10 × 2 is not an adaptation. It is a different exercise, handed
to the child who most needed the original one.
